Title: Sparse Routing Adapters for Low-Resource Translation

Abstract
We study how to adapt a pretrained translation model to language pairs with fewer than
50k parallel sentences. Full fine-tuning overfits quickly in this regime, and plain adapters
leave accuracy on the table.

1. Introduction
Low-resource translation remains hard because most parameters of a large model are tuned
for high-resource pairs. We ask whether a small routed set of adapters can recover most of
the gap at a fraction of the cost.

2. Method
We propose Sparse Routing Adapters (SRA): each transformer layer gets 8 small bottleneck
adapters and a learned router that activates the top-2 adapters per token. Only the adapters
and routers are trained; the base model stays frozen. Training uses label smoothing 0.1 and
a routing balance loss weighted 0.01.

3. Datasets
We evaluate on FLORES-200 (Nepali-English, Sinhala-English) and on the IWSLT 2017
Kazakh-English test set.

4. Metrics
We report chrF++ and BLEU, averaged over three seeds.

5. Results
SRA reaches 41.2 chrF++ on Nepali-English, +2.7 over plain adapters and within 0.4 of full
fine-tuning, while training 3.1% of the parameters.

6. Limitations
Routing adds 6% inference latency. We did not test pairs with more than 1M sentences.
